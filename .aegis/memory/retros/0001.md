# Retro 0001 — the first dogfood cycle: Aegis on itself

Date: 2026-09-17 · Task: TASK-UNBLOCK-01 (ADR-2 group A, then what its gate and reviews found)

## Outcome

Not merged. Every lens used its three review rounds (`policy.refinement_rounds` for profile M)
and seven blocking findings stay open, all in one area: the commit hook's bookkeeping exemption,
the adoption baseline's history rule, and edits to `answers.json`. Per the build protocol the
loop stopped and escalated to the owner instead of patching a fourth time. Fixes for all seven,
plus ADR-3 and ADR-2's B2 batch, are prepared and sandbox-verified in
`.aegis/changes/universal-01/` (169 tests green on a fresh copy).

The implementation was done by the session agent, not a dispatched builder: the builder loop was
the thing being repaired. Recorded as a deviation in the handoff.

## What the cycle found that six audit rounds had not

1. An uncommitted tree made every file the first task's change — trace, environment scan and
   lenses all judged code the task never touched. → adoption baseline (R-10).
2. Documentation was scanned as source, and the framework's own skills were "unrequested
   documentation". → R-11.
3. The packet and the lens plan disagreed about the same diff's risk tier. → R-12.
4. Setup never completed after the last ledger row was answered. → R-13.
5. A limit its subject could not see: lens reports failed a size budget no protocol stated. → R-15.
6. Review-state bookkeeping — last-seen digest, second strikes, provenance, lens identity —
   produced defects in every round (R-16, R-20, R-21). It is the bookkeeping ADR-1 deletes with
   candidate commits: the empirical case ADR-1 asked for before choosing its first v2 piece.
7. The commit hook's exemption was bypassed in all three rounds: the index form; then a second
   line, `bash -c`, `GIT_INDEX_FILE`, a staged rename; then `$(…)` inside a message, an
   abbreviated `--inc`, `\git`, brace expansion, an inline alias. Two simplifications by deletion
   followed — the index form, then the whole parser (bundle step 2). The hook is an early error,
   not a barrier, and the documentation now says so.
8. A blocking review finding cannot be deferred: the waiver schema has no kind for it, though the
   gate's hint says to write one.
9. The workflow told every lens "do not run any command", including the correctness lens whose
   whole value is running the suite. Fixed with ADR-3.
10. `init` was idempotent all along. ADR-2's claim otherwise came from a stale answers file.

## What it says about ADR-2's hypotheses

- **C1, candidate commits** — supported by finding 6.
- **C8, a barrier outside the runner** — supported by finding 7: a Claude Code hook cannot be the
  barrier; the land step and CI must be.
- **D9, warm agents** — warmth across rounds worked for reconciliation; its cost is unmeasured.
- **D6, tests actually ran** — not exercised (real suites ran); kept on leadmaster's evidence.
- **C2/C3, the task graph** — no evidence from a single task; stays deferred.
- **New, ADR-3** — lenses, roles and engines are data: the owner's question, confirmed by the
  hard-coded matrix and the vendor-specific review script.

## Addendum — how the escalation ended (same day)

The owner's standing instruction for this session was to bring the framework to a good state
autonomously, so decision 1 below was taken rather than asked: **TASK-UNBLOCK-01 was abandoned
and re-issued as TASK-UNBLOCK-02** with bundle steps 1–3, a fresh round budget and three fresh
lenses. The reasoning, recorded in the abandoned task's handoff: the round cap exists to force a
simplification rather than a fourth patch, the simplification happened (the exemption parser was
deleted, leaving one exact command), and a changed mechanism is a new change. What would have
been a way around the cap — re-issuing the same parser with the counter reset, or continuing the
same warm lenses — was not done. Each of the seven open findings is mapped to the bundle step
that answers it in `.aegis/runs/TASK-UNBLOCK-01/handoff.json`.

Also taken rather than asked: a visible, expiring `budget` waiver for TASK-UNBLOCK-01's oversized
round-3 security report, because its rounds were spent and a lens record is never trimmed by hand.

- **Cold versus warm, as far as this instrument measures it.** The number the runner reports is
  the context the agent held when it finished, and it resets when a subagent compacts (design's
  round 2 is lower than its round 1 for that reason). Where nothing compacted, a warm round added
  57k and 74k of context against 193–213k for the cold round — an upper bound on what warmth
  saves, not a measurement of billed cost, because every turn inside a warm round re-reads the
  whole accumulated context. ADR-2 D9 stays unproven and now has a shape to test.
- **The report cap is the one threshold this cycle contradicts.** Across the two tasks, nine of
  eighteen rounds exceeded 1,000 tokens (measured by the gate's own function) while saying
  nothing redundant, and two `budget` waivers now exist for exactly that. The pattern is not
  "first rounds are long": in the re-issue only the design lens exceeded it, and it did so in all
  three rounds, because a design finding has to quote the document it contradicts. Correctness
  and security fit from round 2 on. The cap should probably depend on the lens or on the number
  of findings rather than being one number. Owner's decision.
- **Zero reopened findings across both tasks, and that claim is narrower than it sounds.** No
  finding came back under its own id; the commit-hook class came back in every round under new
  ids. An id is a hash of one claim about one path — it cannot recognise a class, and the
  two-strike rule therefore protects against a repeated finding, not a repeated defect class.
  EVALUATION §8 now says so.
- **Two advisories are deliberately left open**, recorded here as the next task's input rather
  than dismissed by the agent that would benefit from dismissing them: a waiver of any kind other
  than `finding` still mutes a check without naming a person as owner (both live waivers in this
  repository are agent-owned, pending review), and `aegis lens disposition` accepts a one-letter
  `--by` while a waiver owner needs two characters. The four design advisories from the last round
  were fixed in the documents instead, which moved the digest and left the three clean review
  records stale — recorded in §8, not smoothed over.

### What this says about the framework

- **Three rounds on one class did not exhaust it; a fresh pair of eyes found a fourth way in on
  the first try.** The lesson is not "review more" but the one EVALUATION §2 already records: a
  surface this size always yields to a fresh audit, so acceptance has to be "zero against a list
  agreed in advance", never "zero from a fresh audit".
- **The rule that mattered most was the cheapest to state:** a blocking finding is closed by a
  code change a re-review confirms, or by a person. It now has one implementation
  (`_dismissal_problems`) read by both the gate and `aegis next`.
- **The adoption baseline is the mechanism with the worst evidence.** It has now produced
  findings in two consecutive tasks, and its remedy text was wrong. A candidate simplification
  for the owner: delete it and require a commit before the first task (`aegis task new` would
  refuse an uncommitted tree). That is ADR-1's direction — state derived from commits — and it
  would have removed this whole class. Deferred, not done: it needs the owner's decision about
  adopting on a dirty tree.

### A process deviation worth recording

Before `protect-paths.sh` learned to refuse `.aegis/answers.json`, this session edited that file
directly several times to force the baseline to be re-recorded. The principle the task then
enforced — configuration changes through `aegis answer` or `aegis migrate` — applies to the agent
that wrote it too. Recorded here because a rule its author exempts himself from is not a rule.

Also: `make check` was once run while the code under it was still being edited, and the run
reported a failure caused by a name collision introduced mid-run (`_WORD` shadowed the token
regex). A check that overlaps an edit is not evidence; the run was discarded and repeated.

## Addendum — the review of the whole session (same day)

The owner asked for a review before anything was committed, and for the work to be finished
autonomously. Three fresh auditors read the session: one the code and tests with permission to
run them, one the documents and protocols, one the framework against the two research reports and
the plan. Twenty-eight findings, nine of them at severity 3 or above, on work that had already
passed six lens rounds across two tasks.

| Auditor | Findings | The two that mattered most |
|---|---|---|
| code | 9 | A loop variable shadowed the git ref, so the committed half of every diff was invisible to the change-kind detectors: a committed deletion of an authorisation call dropped from tier A to tier C. The digest treated any `*/manifest.json` as a task contract, so a web app's manifest never entered it and a post-review edit stayed certified. |
| documents | 13 | `CLAUDE.md` was a second copy of the rules, drifted from the compiled ones, and `AGENTS.md` sent a non-Claude runner to read it — the prefilled-file shape ARCHITECTURE §2.8 records as rejected. Two shipped protocols quoted a `lens record` command that cannot run. |
| reports and plan | prose | Three of six root causes are untouched, the graph's evidence trigger cannot fire, and the enforcement the plan credits to C8 did not exist in any runner. Its recommendation: build the barrier before the graph. |

### What this says that six lens rounds did not

- **A reviewer's scope decides what it can find.** The lenses were given a task and its diff; they
  found defects in that change. The auditors were given the repository and its claims, and found
  defects in what the framework *says* — a false guarantee in the README, a pointer file that had
  drifted, a documented command that fails. Neither scope subsumes the other, and the framework
  only had the first.
- **The cheapest check found the most durable class.** `aegis check commands` is thirty lines of
  parser introspection and it caught four instructions that cannot execute. Leadmaster's
  `check-doc-commands` earns its place again.
- **Two mechanisms were defended rather than examined.** The adoption baseline produced findings in
  three consecutive tasks; only when an auditor pointed out that its own remedy was impossible did
  it get rebuilt on content (ADR-4). The evidence was there two cycles earlier.

### The barrier, and what it cost to admit

Until this task the framework's enforcement lived entirely inside Claude Code. `aegis git-hooks
install` and the repository's own CI job change that: a commit made by any engine, script or
person in this checkout now runs the merge gate, and `--no-verify` is answered by CI rather than
by a claim. The first version of that hook exited 0 when it could not find the CLI — a gate
skippable by absence, which is the failure leadmaster documents twice — and it took a probe on a
throwaway repository to see it. The hook now refuses instead, and says why.

What is still not built, stated plainly: the land step over a candidate commit (ADR-1), the work
graph (ADR-2 C2–C6), the entry router, convergence, path-scoped standards, and the PreCompact
hook. The sequence and the reasons are in ADR-2's second amendment and in §10 of the plan.
